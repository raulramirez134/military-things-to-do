New Arrival Guide — Fort Bragg

First weeks at Fort Bragg,
step by step.

A general checklist for getting settled — order isn't rigid, and dates depend on your unit, but this is roughly how it goes for most families arriving stateside.

Before you arrive
  • Request a sponsor as soon as you get orders — complete DA Form 5434 through Army Career Tracker so your gaining unit can assign one.Your sponsor is genuinely one of the most useful resources for what's changed recently and current wait times.
  • Start your household goods paperwork early through the Defense Personal Property System (DPS) — schedule your move as soon as your dates are firm.
  • Check your state driver's license is REAL ID compliant — Fort Bragg enforces the REAL ID Act, and a non-compliant license may require extra ID at the gate.
First week
  • Report to the XVIII Airborne Corps Reception Company if you're a first-time arrival to Fort Bragg — it's a 24-hour operation for permanent party soldiers, then check in with your unit's S-1 (personnel office).
  • Enroll in DEERS and update ID cards for every family member at the ID card section in the Soldier Support Center.
  • Visit the Corvias Housing Office if you're planning on-post housing — call ahead at (910) 396-1022, and apply as early as possible since waitlists move slowly.
  • Start your off-post housing search early if renting — Fayetteville, Hope Mills, and Spring Lake are the most affordable areas; Southern Pines/Pinehurst costs more but is popular for schools.
First month
  • Register your vehicle and get a North Carolina driver's license if you're relocating from out of state — the NC DMV has its own timeline requirements for new residents.
  • Enroll kids in school or the CDC/childcare waitlist as early as possible — check whether you're zoned for Cumberland County Schools or Harnett/Moore County depending on where you land.See the Schools & Childcare section on the main listings page.
  • Register with TRICARE and get assigned a Primary Care Manager through Womack Army Medical Center so routine and urgent care are set up before you need them.
  • Set up on-base or local banking and confirm your BAH is calculating correctly for the Fort Bragg/Pope Military Housing Area (MHA code NC182).
Settling in
  • Find a spouses' club or Family Readiness Group — one of the fastest ways to build a local support network, especially given how often units deploy or rotate.
  • Explore beyond the post — the Fayetteville area and nearby day trips (Asheville, the Outer Banks) have a lot to offer once the paperwork phase is behind you.
