New Arrival Guide — Yokota AB

First weeks at Yokota,
step by step.

A general checklist for getting settled — order isn't rigid, and dates depend on your sponsor and unit, but this is roughly how it goes for most families.

Before you arrive
  • Apply for a Special Issuance Passport for every family member as soon as you get orders — everyone living at Yokota needs one to enter Japan, and the process takes real lead time.Family members without a passport in hand before your final out won't be eligible for military benefits at Yokota.
  • Confirm your sponsor through the e-Sponsorship Application and Training (eSAT) system — they'll help arrange a mailbox and temporary lodging and are your best source of current, on-the-ground information.
  • Check pet import requirements for Japan early if you're bringing one — restrictions and lead times apply, and your sponsor can point you to current rules.
  • Start your household goods paperwork early — separate shipments (household goods, unaccompanied baggage) often arrive on very different timelines overseas.
First week
  • Clear customs and connect with your sponsor at arrival — most newcomers arrive via the AMC terminal on the Patriot Express; if you fly into Narita instead, arrange transport to Yokota with your sponsor ahead of time.
  • Check in with Personnel, Finance, Housing, and Medical — most new arrivals complete initial in-processing within 2–3 business days with their sponsor's help scheduling appointments.
  • Attend the 2-day Right Start Newcomers Orientation at the Airman & Family Readiness Center (Bldg. 535) — mandatory, and covers medical in-processing, driver's licensing, and cultural adaptation. Spouses should attend too.
  • Borrow household items from the Family Services Loan Closet while your shipment is in transit — a copy of your orders is required, and supplies run thin during peak PCS season (May–August).
First month
  • Get your Japanese driving permit sorted through Right Start — driving here means adjusting to left-side driving, and the licensing process is covered as part of your mandatory in-processing briefings.
  • Decide between East Side and West Side housing if living on base — East Side suits junior enlisted families with townhomes near the youth center and elementary school; West Side has larger single-family homes.
  • Coordinate with the Traffic Management Office (TMO) early on any unique customs paperwork or restricted items in your household goods shipment.
  • Register with TRICARE Overseas so routine and urgent care are already set up before you need them.
  • Register kids for school or childcare waitlists as early as possible — waitlists move slowly, especially during peak PCS season.See the Schools & Childcare section on the main listings page.
Settling in
  • Take the basic Japanese phrases course through the Military & Family Readiness Center — even a little goes a long way for daily errands off base.See the Language Schools section on the main listings page.
  • Find a spouses' club or community group — one of the fastest ways to build a local support network so far from home.
  • Explore beyond the base — Tokyo, Mt. Fuji, and the surrounding area have a lot to offer once the paperwork phase is behind you.
