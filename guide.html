New Arrival Guide — Ramstein / KMC

First weeks at Ramstein,
step by step.

A general checklist for getting settled — order isn't rigid, and dates depend on your sponsor and unit, but this is roughly how it goes for most families.

Before you arrive
  • Confirm your sponsor is assigned and in contact — they're your best source for what's changed recently.Ask about your report-no-later-than date and any current in-processing quirks.
  • Start your household goods paperwork early — separate shipments (household goods, unaccompanied baggage) often arrive on very different timelines overseas.
  • Check passport and visa status for every family member, including pets if you're bringing one — pet import rules for Germany have real lead time requirements.
First week
  • In-process with your unit and get your Geneva Conventions ID/dependent ID cards sorted at the ID card section.
  • Visit the Housing Office if you're planning to live off base, before signing anything — they can flag known problem landlords or areas.
  • Register with the Visitor Control Center process if any family members will need access without an active sponsor present.
  • Stop by the Commissary and Exchange to get oriented — small thing, but it helps to know where the basics are early.
First month
  • Get your USAREUR/local driving license through the Automobile Registration process — required before driving off base, and it takes a written test.
  • Register your vehicle and sort out German liability insurance if you're bringing or buying a car locally.
  • Get your ration card and tax-relief (VAT) paperwork sorted — this affects fuel purchases and off-base big purchases.
  • Set up on-base banking or confirm your stateside bank handles overseas transactions and ATM fees reasonably.
  • Register kids for school or the CDC/childcare waitlist as early as possible — waitlists move slowly.See the Schools & Childcare section on the main listings page.
  • Register with the on-base clinic or TRICARE Overseas so routine and urgent care are already set up before you need them.
Settling in
  • Look into a German language class — even basic phrases make daily errands easier.See the Language Schools section on the main listings page.
  • Find a spouses' club or community group — one of the fastest ways to build a local support network.
  • Explore beyond the base — the KMC has a lot to offer once the paperwork phase is behind you.
